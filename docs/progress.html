How a piece clears

  • Operability ≥ 80% drivable
  • Zero P0 defects open
  • at most two P1, each owned
  • every fix has a test that fails without it

The first gate asked a critic to pick us over Stripe with zero blocking issues. That is unsatisfiable for real software, so it printed "reference wins" 25 times running and told us nothing. It now measures Operability — can a human actually complete the job on screen — and Defect Debt, both of which move.

The end-to-end suite runs in CI, and it earned its place on the first run: the dashboard was printing "Monthly recurring revenue" twice from two different queries, and Escape could throw away an inline edit in the frame before focus reached the calendar. Both are fixed at the cause. Three figures that looked right were not — a credit balance that stopped counting at 500 grants, an invoice drain that deferred a customer's remainder, and a mistyped filter that came back as the whole list. And the copilot's scope reconciliation is gone: 2,548 lines the answer path stopped calling a wave ago, kept alive only by its own tests.

Wave 12 · The browser suite in CI, and what it caught

5/5

Putting the end-to-end suite in front of CI rather than beside it. Every defect below was found by software driving the product, not by reading it — and each fix ships a test run against the pre-fix code to confirm it fails there.

  • The end-to-end suite runs on every push

    Cleared 100% debt 0 1 round

    396 tests in one worker against one database, on its own CI job. The per-principal limiter is 600 requests a real minute and one worker runs into it; a refused read is kept rather than retried, so whichever screen was loading when the bucket emptied drew nothing for the rest of that test — which reads as a product defect and is not one. The limit is raised for the run rather than worked around in specs. The job also checks the server is still alive afterwards, because a preview that died turns every remaining test red for one reason.

  • Two tiles, one name

    Cleared 100% debt 0 1 round

    The dashboard drew "Monthly recurring revenue" twice — its own tile from the subscriptions headline, the revenue module's widget from the twelve-month series — with no way for a reader to tell which figure was which. The headline belongs to the module that draws it with its trend and its retention; billing's tile now says what billing knows and revenue does not, which is how many subscriptions are live. The guard that should have caught it read only labels written as string literals, and the first fix had made this one a conditional expression, so it passed over the very tile it was written for.

  • Escape stops throwing away the edit

    Cleared 100% debt 0 1 round

    Focus reaches a freshly opened overlay a frame after it opens, and every overlay bound Escape on the document in the bubble phase — after React, which attaches at the root container. In that frame the keystroke still belonged to whatever was focused before, so opening a date calendar on an inline row and pressing Escape straight away discarded the whole edit. Which frame won depended on the machine, which is the shape of a race and not of a flake. Overlays claim Escape in capture when it comes from outside themselves and leave it to bubble when it comes from inside, so a search field clearing itself keeps first refusal.

  • Three figures that stopped counting

    Cleared 100% debt 0 1 round

    The credits overview folded a page of 500 grants and reported it as the whole book, so a workspace holding more was told it held less by a figure that looked exactly like a right one. The invoice drain claimed 500 pending lines and left a customer's remainder for whenever the next bill happened to be drawn. And an unknown query parameter was dropped in silence — an unknown body parameter has always been refused — so ?statuss=open returned every row looking like a filter that matched them all. That is how a missing dunning filter hid earlier in the build.

  • The scope reconciliation is retired

    Cleared 100% debt 0 1 round

    2,548 lines that once caught an engine which parsed a qualifier out of a question, failed to bind it, and stated the unqualified result as the answer. The engine is a template whitelist now — it binds every slot or refuses — so there is no unbound qualifier left to catch, and the answer card was rebuilt around that a wave ago. The suite said so itself, in a test whose comment read "the unplugged machinery … kept as a library for the board's tests; nothing on the answer path calls it", and which flagged a correct answer when run.

Wave 13 · Judged against live software

0/6

Fourteen fresh critics drove the running product rather than reading the build's own account of itself, and every P0/P1 they raised went to an adversarial verifier told to refute it. Ten of fifty-nine agents' findings were refuted and dropped. What survived is below: the engines are strong and the seams between them were never built.

  • The modules were never made into one product

    Sent back 65% debt 30 1 round

    Billing does not read entitlements — grep finds zero references across billing, metering and credits — so the included allowance the catalogue sells and the Features screen displays is decorative. The flagship account is over-billed by about $7,400 in a single period, under a screen captioned "never out of step with the bill". Following one company across every surface, the engines each work and the seams do not: a company record with no path to its money, a $127,840 receivable no collections surface knows about.

  • One password, every workspace

    Sent back 60% debt 30 1 round

    Per-object isolation and RBAC are genuinely strong: no cross-tenant read or write, no role escalation, removal cascades revoke sessions and descendant keys. But identity is a single global user row, and the invitation-accept flow lets any workspace admin confirm a platform user by email, read their PII, and set that shared password — which is a login into every other workspace that user belongs to. Strong walls around each object do not matter if you can walk in as the owner.

  • Nothing ever leaves the building

    Sent back 50% debt 20 1 round

    No email, webhook, PDF, hosted page or outbound anything — while the product says otherwise in shipped copy. Every draft invoice reads "Finalise it to send it"; finalising sends nothing. Dunning retries a card on days 3, 5 and 7 and never tells the payer. Settings → Events offers to show "exactly what a webhook would receive" over a feature with no endpoint, no delivery and no table. The AI writes a chase letter whose only button is Log.

  • There is no discount, anywhere

    Sent back 0% debt 10 1 round

    No coupon, promotion code or discount exists in the server; the invoice has no discount line and the subscription no discount. The id prefixes are reserved and unbuilt. A shipped refusal message tells the operator to "discount with a coupon" — a feature that does not exist. Nothing downstream can express "20% off year one", which every negotiated contract in the demo's own pipeline would carry.

  • The estimate is not the bill

    Sent back 90% debt 3 1 round

    Everything that actually charges money reconciles to the cent — proration, tiering, credit burn, tax, credit notes, MRR — audited longhand against the product's own figures. But the upcoming-invoice preview is a second, poorer implementation that drops accrued metered usage and committed proration: $99.00 shown against $3,019.00 actually coming, on a usage-priced product where most subscriptions carry a metered item.

  • Nothing checks that a claimed mechanism exists

    Sent back 76% debt 9 1 round

    2,384 tests, an in-process smoke of every GET route, a reconciliation that can genuinely fail, and static guards against a seventh status vocabulary — every one of them checks a value, a label or a vocabulary. Not one asks whether there is code behind a sentence the product says on screen. That is exactly why the architectural falsehoods survived while the numeric ones did not.
