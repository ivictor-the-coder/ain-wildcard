How a piece clears

  • Operability ≥ 80% drivable
  • Zero P0 defects open
  • at most two P1, each owned
  • every fix has a test that fails without it

The first gate asked a critic to pick us over Stripe with zero blocking issues. That is unsatisfiable for real software, so it printed "reference wins" 25 times running and told us nothing. It now measures Operability — can a human actually complete the job on screen — and Defect Debt, both of which move.

Wave 9 re-judged the server against the database. The template engine’s only P0 in 458 questions was one optional word; revenue’s history was being rewritten by un-prorated plan changes; payments wrote off a 3DS card in a millisecond; billing billed usage a cycle late. All fixed with fail-before-pass tests, and a coherence pass removed every duplicated screen, trail and palette entry. Next: re-judge the fixes, then the client surfaces.

Wave 2 · Revenue

3/6

Two dependency chains in parallel: customers/subscriptions to invoicing to payments to revenue, alongside metering/credits to entitlements. Every critic attacks the arithmetic first.

  • Customers, subscriptions & proration

    Cleared 100% debt 0 6 rounds

    Anchor-day cycles, trials, schedules, and one proration function shared by preview and charge — so a preview cannot drift from the bill. An auditor could not make it lose a cent across 800 days and four currencies.

  • Usage metering & prepaid credits

    Cleared 100% debt 0 5 rounds

    Exactly-once ingestion, incremental rollups, an append-only credit ledger, and a documented burn-down order.

  • Invoicing, credit notes & tax

    In review 90% debt 6 3 rounds

    Every one of 43 invoices reconciled to the cent across stacked, inclusive, reverse-charged, prorated and credited cases. Two P1s fixed: metered usage now lands on the renewal that names its window (the bill waits for the settlement it says it settles), and a cancellation sweeps every pending proration into a final invoice. Open: the subscriptions overview still adds euros to dollars in its headline — a change that crosses three owners.

    CriticThe invoice arithmetic is Stripe-grade, but usage billed a month late, cancelled subscriptions left prorations unbilled, and the overview added euros to dollars.

  • Payments, retries & dunning

    In review 96% debt 4 3 rounds

    199 invoices, every ledger and every schedule reconciled to the row. Three P1s fixed: a card that needs its holder is held in past_due for the window the retries would have run in, not written off in a millisecond; a refund that reopens a paid bill hands it to a campaign; the do-not-retry family (stolen, lost, pickup, fraudulent, restricted) exists and is never presented twice.

    CriticThe money never lies here, but the recovery policy was harsher and narrower than Stripe’s: a 3DS card written off in a millisecond, a refunded bill owed by nobody, and no card-stolen family of declines.

  • Entitlements & feature gating

    Cleared 100% debt 0 2 rounds

    Features, limits and live usage-backed checks with a real upgrade path.

  • Revenue reporting

    In review 67% debt 6 3 rounds

    Three P0s fixed: contract history is read from the event log so a schedule phase no longer rewrites closed months (Halstead’s April stays 9,900); credit notes reduce recognised and deferred from the day they are issued; usage "charged" is invoice money, not settlement-ledger money. A collection pause is a pause, not churn. Open: the module’s three copilot tools are not reachable from any template.

    CriticThe arithmetic on today’s book is exact, but the history underneath it was not durable: one un-prorated plan change rewrote five closed months, credit notes never reached recognition, and usage "charged" was money no invoice carried.
